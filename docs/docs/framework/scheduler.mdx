# The scheduler

File: `robomorph/src/core/scheduler.rs`.

> **Vocabulary.** A **processes pipeline** (`ProcessesPipeline`) is the scheduler's execution unit: an ordered list of processes sharing one thread and one clock, linked by the pipe. It is not a **robotics pipeline**, a functional domain of the stack such as [motor control](../pipelines/motor-control/overview.mdx). A robotics pipeline runs on one or more processes pipelines. Until the rename, the type was called `ProcessesChain`.

## The `Process` trait

A `Process` is the unit of scheduling. Its core method is:

```rust
fn exec(&mut self, input: &Option<AnyMessage>, dt: Duration) -> Option<AnyMessage>;
```

- `input` is the **pipe**: the value returned by the process registered **right before** this one, in the same pass. It is `None` when that process abstained or was not executed in this pass.
- The returned value becomes the `input` of the next process. `Some(...)` hands something over; `None` means *nothing to hand over* — an explicit abstention.
- `dt` is the **measured** time since this process last started its task: about one period in steady state, more after a skipped tick. At the first execution it is the nominal period.

The remaining methods (`set_receiver`, `set_sender`, `set_period_from_freq`, `get_period`, `set_name`, `get_name`) are wiring.

## `ProcessesPipeline`

A processes pipeline is an ordered list of `(process, next due instant, last start)`. `run_once`:

1. computes `end_instant`, the latest of the `due instant + period` deadlines;
2. makes **passes** over the processes, in registration order, and sleeps between two passes until the nearest due instant;
3. in a pass, executes each process that is due. The pipe goes from a process to the next one only: it is emptied at the start of every pass and whenever a process is skipped;
4. after an execution, advances the process's due instant by **whole periods** until it is in the future: this is its **grid**. A late process skips the ticks it missed and keeps its phase;
5. tests `end_instant` **after the pass**, never between two processes: a pass that has started always runs to its end.

A process whose period is **zero** has no grid. It follows its predecessor: it runs in every pass where the process before it ran. A zero-period **first** process runs once per `run_once` call, in its first pass.

In steady state, with every process at the same period, a call makes one pass where nothing is due, then two passes where all the processes run, one per tick.

### What the pipeline guarantees

1. **A producer and the consumer registered right after it share every pass.** The pass is never cut between them (step 5), and they stay on the same grid (step 4). The pipe between them is therefore reliable by construction, whatever the execution times.
2. **A process only receives what its immediate predecessor produced in that pass.** If the predecessor did not run, the input is `None`: the pipe never jumps over a process. A consumer that needs a value at every pass keeps the last one itself, as the mixer keeps its last wrench.
3. **`dt` is the real elapsed time.** The mixer bounds each arm's motion per cycle with `max_rot_speed × dt`. This is **wall-clock** time: under Gazebo, physics runs on simulated time, and the two drift apart when Gazebo is slower than real time. An integrator has to take this into account (velocity loop, brick 5).
4. **An empty processes pipeline returns at once**, with nothing to sleep for: a thread that loops on it spins and burns a whole core. Never start a processes pipeline with no process in it.

## Incident: the open attitude loop (2026-10-13)

### What was seen

On the first Gazebo run of the scenario bench (`roll_step`), the motor commands **stayed frozen to the sixth decimal for 1.8 s** while the vehicle slowly drifted: 1.5° of roll, 7° of yaw. With the loop open, the vehicle ended up flipping over.

A second run, with the console output piped away, held a perfect hover (0.145° of tilt) but **ignored the 10° step for 0.34 s**. The commanded roll moment followed the attitude law in **0 %** of the passes just after the step, then 29 %, then 0 % again, against 100 % in hover.

### Why the hover looked healthy

In a calm hover, the demanded wrench barely changes from one pass to the next: re-applying an old wrench is almost right, and a freeze is invisible. A freeze only shows when the demand changes, on a step or a disturbance. Rung C of the [validation](../pipelines/motor-control/validation.mdx) only tested a calm hover.

### How it was found

The search went by elimination along the chain:
- **sensors → bridge**: the recorder reads the same pose stream as the attitude stage, and the roll did change;
- **attitude law + mixer**: replayed offline with the bridge's exact geometry, they commanded the right moment;
- **what was left was the transport**, i.e. the pipe from the attitude stage to the mixer. A replica of `run_once` with dummy processes then reproduced the loss, but only once execution-time jitter was added.

### The trap

Three defects of the former `run_once`, each harmless alone:

1. the pipe was a variable recreated **empty at every pass**;
2. `end_instant` was tested **before each process**, so a pass could be cut in the middle;
3. a late process had its due instant **reset to now**, with no memory of the grid.

Together they formed a stable trap:

1. A ~1 ms hiccup made the mixer — the longest process — finish late once.
2. Defect 3 moved its due instant ~1.4 ms behind the others, **for good**: afterwards it advanced by one period at a time, like every other process, and nothing ever pulled it back.
3. Being the latest due instant, it now set `end_instant` itself.
4. At every tick, the walk reached the mixer just after its own due instant, that is, just after the deadline: defect 2 cut the pass right after the attitude stage, and defect 1 dropped the wrench with the pipe.
5. In the next call, the mixer ran alone with an empty input and re-applied its old wrench.
6. The phases no longer moving, the same cut happened at every tick until another hiccup reshuffled them: freezes from a fraction of a second up to 1.8 s.

The hiccups came from the **console**. The bridge's sensor callbacks printed thousands of lines per second (`joint_state` up to 1 kHz), some of them while holding the locks the control thread needs. A `println!` blocks when the terminal cannot keep up, and the control thread then waited for those locks.

### The fix (`2681708`)

Steps 4 and 5 of the algorithm above: the grid removes the lasting phase shift, and the complete pass removes the cut. The strict pipe (step 3) and the measured `dt` came with the same commit. Since then, the bridge prints nothing by default, and never while holding a lock it shares with the control thread (see [Conventions](conventions.mdx#period-budget)).

| Wrench received by the mixer | Moderate jitter | Heavy jitter |
|---|---|---|
| replica of the former `run_once` | 94.5 % | 57.9 % |
| replica, grid only | 99.9 % | 93.9 % |
| replica, grid + complete pass | 100 % | 100 % |
| **real scheduler, before the fix** | **91.3 %** | **24.0 %** |
| **real scheduler, after the fix** | **100 %** | **100 %** |

In Gazebo after the fix, the commanded roll moment followed the attitude law in **100 %** of the passes, including during the step.

## `Scheduler`

- `register_process` → **main pipeline**, run on the caller's thread by `run_main_pipeline()` (the binary calls it in a `loop`).
- `register_side_process(p, id)` → **side pipeline** `id`, run on a dedicated thread by `start_all_side_pipelines()`.
- `register_interface` / `start_all_interfaces` → `HardwareInterface`s are *connected* (they own their threads); they are not scheduled.

### Architectural rule: one processes pipeline, one clock

A producer and its consumer must live **in the same processes pipeline**. Two processes pipelines at the same nominal frequency on two threads drift in phase and produce empty and double ticks — a beat, measured at 30 empty / 30 double ticks out of 454 before the pose producer joined the controllers' processes pipeline. Side pipelines are for decoupled work (heavy perception), not for the links of a control loop.

## Ordering and loop delay

The order `osprai → attitude → mixer` makes the attitude stage consume a **fresh** measurement, published moments earlier in the same pass, and applies the mixer's commands **on the next pass**. Every discrete controller pays a computation delay somewhere; placing it on actuation rather than on measurement is the right choice for a critical loop, and it is covered by the `τ ≥ 10·T` margin.
